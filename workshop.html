Before we build

A word about your own philosophy of AI

AI is a complicated topic, and an hour isn’t enough time to do justice to the ethics of it. I’d encourage you to spend some time away from the tools developing your own philosophy of AI, the same way many of us wrote teaching philosophies early in our careers. That statement is worth writing because it shows you what you believe, and it should change as you tinker.

The Wish List

Set aside what’s practical for a few minutes. These prompts ask what the people you work with need from the materials you give them and where those materials fall short. If you teach, think about your students. If you work in advising, the library, tutoring, or another office, think about the students or colleagues who come to you.

An aside on branding

A style sheet keeps every build consistent

I give the tool a style sheet at the start of every build: colors with hex codes, typefaces and what each is for, a logo, and a motto. Every new page then starts out looking like it belongs to the course.

Same wish, two prompts

Before and after

Before: Make a quiz about thesis statements.

After: I teach first-year composition at a community college, and many of my students work or are the first in their families to attend college. Build a short practice activity that helps them tell a strong thesis statement from a weak one. Our textbook explains thesis statements but never asks students to judge one, so this should be practice, not explanation. Show one thesis at a time, have students decide whether it is strong, and tell them why in plain language, including when they got it right. I want them to leave feeling that someone anticipated where they’d get stuck. Make it a single HTML file that works on a phone and meets WCAG AA. Before you build it, tell me your plan and ask me anything you need to know.
